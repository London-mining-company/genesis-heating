### The Problem: Heat Pump Water Heaters

**This is your real threat.** Not Reliance. Not standard tanks. The HPWH.

A heat pump water heater costs $3,600-$6,200 installed AND:
- Uses 60% less electricity than a standard tank
- Qualifies for a **$500 Ontario HRSP rebate** right now
- Has no volatile compute revenue dependency
- Is a known, understood technology
- Every plumber in London can install one

**Your $5,950 Owner Path sits in the SAME price bracket as a heat pump water heater, but with significantly more complexity and risk.**

A CPA would tell you this: a homeowner comparing your system to a HPWH will see:
- HPWH: $5,700 installed - $500 rebate = **$5,200 net. Saves $550/year. Zero risk.**
- Genesis: $5,950 installed. Saves ~$1,000/year. **Dependent on BTC price. Unknown brand.**

The $450/year delta in your favour is NOT enough to overcome the trust gap.

### How You Win Against HPWHs

1. **You don't compete on energy savings alone.** HPWHs save money by using less energy. You save money by using the SAME energy but getting paid for it. Your pitch is: "A heat pump saves you $550/year. We can earn you $1,000-$2,000/year. The heat pump is the safer choice. We're the smarter one."

2. **Your Builder Path ($450) destroys the HPWH value proposition.** Nobody offers a $450 water heater that also generates income. This is your killer product. Lead with it against HPWH comparisons.

3. **HPWHs have a dirty secret:** They pull heat from the surrounding air. In a Canadian basement in January, that means they're cooling an already cold space. Some homeowners report their furnace running harder to compensate. Your system GENERATES heat — it warms the basement.

## SECTION 2: TAX & INCENTIVE ANALYSIS (Ontario/Canada 2026)

### For Residential Customers

| Incentive | Applies to Genesis? | Amount | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|--------|-------|
| **Ontario HRSP - Heat Pump WH Rebate** | **UNLIKELY** | $500 | Requires ENERGY STAR qualified heat pump water heater. The Superheat H1 is not a heat pump. Unless Superheat gets this certification, this rebate does NOT apply to you. |
| **Canada Greener Homes Affordability** | **NO** | Varies | Program targets low-to-median income, specific equipment list. Computational water heaters are not listed. |
| **Federal Carbon Tax Rebate** | **INDIRECT** | Varies | Your system doesn't directly qualify, but you can market the 4.2 ton CO2 offset as a selling point. |

**CPA Verdict (Residential):** You are NOT eligible for any existing residential water heater incentive programs. The Superheat H1 is not classified as a heat pump or ENERGY STAR water heater. **Do not claim or imply government rebate eligibility in any marketing.**

### For Business/Commercial Customers — THIS IS WHERE THE GOLD IS

| Incentive | Applies to Genesis? | Amount | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|--------|-------|
| **CCA Class 43.1 (30% declining)** | **POSSIBLE** | 30% CCA/yr | If the system qualifies as "energy conservation equipment." Requires CRA ruling or accountant letter. |
| **CCA Class 43.2 (50% declining)** | **POSSIBLE** | 50% CCA/yr | Higher rate for equipment acquired before 2025. Post-2025 may revert to Class 43.1. |
| **Immediate Expensing (100%)** | **POSSIBLE** | Full write-off Year 1 | For clean energy equipment in Classes 43.1/43.2 acquired 2025-2029. If the H1 qualifies, a business could write off the ENTIRE $5,950 in Year 1. |
| **Clean Technology ITC** | **POSSIBLE** | 30% refundable credit | Up to 30% of capital cost for "clean technology property" including heat recovery systems. A $5,950 unit could yield a $1,785 refundable tax credit. |
| **Small Business Deduction** | **YES** | Standard | The compute rewards are business income. Operating expenses (electricity) are deductible. |

**CPA Verdict (Commercial):** A business owner could potentially:
1. **Write off 100%** of the $5,950 purchase price in Year 1 (immediate expensing)
2. **Claim a 30% refundable ITC** ($1,785) under the Clean Tech credit
3. **Deduct electricity** as a business operating expense
4. **Report compute rewards** as business income (after expenses, likely near-zero tax impact)

**Effective cost to a business:** $5,950 - $1,785 (ITC) = **$4,165 net.** With immediate expensing, the after-tax cost drops further depending on marginal rate.

**THIS IS YOUR ENTERPRISE ANGLE.** A restaurant, salon, gym, or small manufacturer with high hot water usage could install a Genesis system and write off the entire thing.

## SECTION 5: THE CPA'S FINAL WORD

### What Would Kill This Business

1. **BTC drops below $30k for 12+ months.** Your Builder Path units go cash-flow negative. You owe nothing, but your customers earn nothing, and word of mouth turns toxic.

2. **Superheat raises hardware prices or restricts resale.** You have zero contractual protection right now. Get a written agreement.

3. **A competitor with capital enters London.** If Reliance or Enercare decides to offer a similar product with their existing 500,000+ customer base, your neighbourhood density strategy becomes irrelevant. **Speed is your only moat until first installs are live.**

4. **CRA audits compute rewards as personal income.** If customers get a surprise tax bill on their "credits," trust evaporates. **Proactively provide the tax advisory document.**

### What Would Make This Business Succeed

1. **Get the first install done, tracked, and publicized.** Real data from a real London home silences every objection.

2. **Target 2-3 commercial deals early.** One restaurant deal ($12k-$18k) funds your first 3 Builder Path units.

3. **Position the Builder Path ($450) as the anchor product.** It's the lowest-friction entry point in the market. Nobody else offers a water heater for $450. That's your hook.

4. **Get a CPA/accountant to validate the CCA/ITC eligibility before making any tax claims.** This is real money (potentially $1,785 per unit in refundable credits for businesses), but you need professional confirmation that the Superheat H1 qualifies under Class 43.1. One call to a tax accountant. Do it before marketing the tax angle.

5. **Never guarantee a specific dollar amount of savings.** "Approximately $1,000/year" with a footnote is your ceiling. The moment you put a specific number without a disclaimer, you have a liability.
